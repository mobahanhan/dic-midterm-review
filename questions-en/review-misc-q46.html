Question

Why should the PMOS\mathrm{PMOS}PMOS to NMOS\mathrm{NMOS}NMOS size ratio in the pseudo NMOS\mathrm{NMOS}NMOS (Pseudo-NMOS) be designed as 0.50.50.5?

Answer and Explanation

0.50.50.5 is not a general law, but a design choice of "weak pull-up, strong pull-down" under a certain process and goal. The original question does not give mobility, channel length, load or Noise margin target, and cannot strictly deduce the unique 0.50.50.5 from these missing data.

The PMOS\mathrm{PMOS}PMOS of the pseudo NMOS\mathrm{NMOS}NMOS is always on. In order to make the low level low enough, the NMOS\mathrm{NMOS}NMOS pull-down must be significantly stronger than the PMOS\mathrm{PMOS}PMOS pull-up. What really controls the flow is the device strength ratio:

If the two transistors are equal in length and use an example approximation of μn/μp≈2\mu_n/\mu_p\approx2μn​/μp​≈2, the width ratio of the corresponding strength to Wp/Wn=0.5W_p/W_n=0.5Wp​/Wn​=0.5 is lower than that of βp/βn≈0.25\beta_p/\beta_n\approx0.25βp​/βn​≈0.25, indicating that PMOS\mathrm{PMOS}PMOS pull-up is significantly weaker. The mobility ratio here is a conditional assumption that explains the design idea, not the given value of the original question.
