Concepts You Need

Parasitic capacitance is an inherent part of the device structure and must be charged and discharged. The gate overlaps the source and drain edges, producing overlap capacitance. The interaction of the gate and channel charge produces channel capacitance. The source and drain diffusions form PN\mathrm{PN}PN junctions with the body and thus have junction capacitance. Cgs,Cgd,CgbC_{gs},C_{gd},C_{gb}Cgs​,Cgd​,Cgb​ connect the gate to the source, drain, and body, respectively. Cdb,CsbC_{db},C_{sb}Cdb​,Csb​ connect the drain and source to the body, respectively. CoxC_{\mathrm{ox}}Cox​ is the gate-oxide capacitance per unit area, and W,L\mathrm{W},LW,L are the channel width and length. The junction bottom area is A\mathrm{A}A, and the sidewall perimeter is PPP. Multiply the bottom capacitance per unit area by A\mathrm{A}A, and multiply the sidewall capacitance per unit length by the corresponding perimeter. When input and output move in opposite directions by equal amounts, the capacitance between them has about twice its nominal value when referred to either node. This is the Miller effect. Original English question

Diffusion (junction) capacitance is voltage-dependent because:

  1. A. The oxide thickness changes with voltage
  2. B. The depletion region width changes with reverse bias
  3. C. The mobility decreases with electric field
  4. D. The threshold voltage shifts with body bias
Show Answer and Explanation

B. Reverse bias changes the depletion width, which changes junction capacitance. The oxide thickness is fixed during this bias sweep.
